Nutrition is only one aspect of health behaviours and the development of these in relation with chronic disease is better conceptualized in a ‘chain of risk’ framework (Kuh et al, 1997). Over the life course, there may be an accumulation of biological and social risk. For instance, poor foetal nutrition (Barker et al, 1990; Barker 1996), in combination with inadequate or adverse influence of parents, peers and the educational experience in childhood and adolescence, sets the stage for chronic disease in adult life, with additional risk coming from cultural influences and lifestyles.
