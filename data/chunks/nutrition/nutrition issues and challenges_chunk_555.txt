In children, the most common allergens are cow’s milk, eggs, fish, peanut, wheat and soy, and in adults, nuts, shellfish and eggs (Chandra 1997). While young infants commonly present with gastrointestinal problems, older children commonly have skin signs. Angioedema and anaphylaxis may occur at any age (Chandra 1997). The mainstay of therapy remains the avoidance of incriminated foods, and education to deal with inadvertent exposure (Sampson 1997).
