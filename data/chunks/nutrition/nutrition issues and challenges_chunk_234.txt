It may be difficult for western adolescents to have a balanced diet, considering that snacking represents an increasing part of their food intake, and that the most popular and widely available snacks are high in sugar or in fat (Bull 1992). Even in Europe (France), fast-food attraction was shown to be very strong among adolescents, but mainly among the youngest, the boys, and the foreign (Chauliac and de Beco, 1996). In developing-country adolescents, eating “on the go” may also be common, and street foods represent a major dietary contributor.
