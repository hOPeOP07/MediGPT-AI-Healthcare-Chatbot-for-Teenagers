However, there is supportive evidence for continuing bone acquisition after the adolescent growth spurt, and calcium intake appears to make a difference, at least in Caucasians (Recker et al, 1992). The window for bone building may remain open for a few years after growth in height has stopped (Heany, 1998), but there may be wide disparities among populations. Calcium deficiency is associated with high post-menopausal bone loss. Regular consumption of dairy products during adolescence was found to be related to lower levels of post-menopausal bone loss, as shown in a retrospective study of Caucasian American women aged 49-66 years old (Sandler et al, 1985).
