Nutritionally at-risk and undernourished children are likely to derive even greater benefits from school breakfast (Pollitt 1995; Simeon 1998), and nutritional status improvements have been reported, in terms of growth or reduction of anaemia (Jacoby, Cueto and Pollitt, 1998; Powell et al, 1998). Eating breakfast may therefore be sound advice for weight management, as well as for nutritional metabolic, and school performance benefits. Brazil is an example of a country in nutritional transition, with increasing rates of obesity and related chronic diseases. In a study of snacking patterns among middle-income adolescents in Manaus, it was found that although high-nutrient-density snack foods were available, adolescents preferred the low nutrient density items.
