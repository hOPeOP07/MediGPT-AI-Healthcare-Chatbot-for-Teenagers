The Minnesota Adolescent health survey conducted in 1986-1987 in the USA on 30,000 adolescents (Neumark-Sztainer et al, 1998) yielded a wealth of information about adolescent nutrition, some of which may have practical applications also in developing countries. This survey provided a unique opportunity to assess the prevalence of nutrition-related items in a large and diverse adolescent population, and to examine their relationship with a range of sociodemographic, psychosocial, and other health-promoting and health- compromising behaviours.
