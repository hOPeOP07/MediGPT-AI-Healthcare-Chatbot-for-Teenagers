The World Bank, in its 1993 Report, identified school health and nutrition programmes as one of the five priority public health initiatives. It supports programmes delivering simple health and nutrition intervention packages. Many development banks, bilateral agencies and NGOs are also involved. The Partnership for Child Development (PCD) was established to conduct and promote operations research on school health and nutrition programmes (Hall and Bundy, 1998). There are currently PCD programmes in 14 countries.
