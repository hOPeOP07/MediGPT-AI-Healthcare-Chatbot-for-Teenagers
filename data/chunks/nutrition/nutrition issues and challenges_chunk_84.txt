Infection as a factor of malnutrition may be relatively less important in adolescents than in under-five children (although adolescents are highly exposed to HIV), while lifestyle factors become more critical. Early pregnancy is a major risk factor in adolescence anywhere. Past malnutrition, or low body nutrient stores, are in the background; they may have consequences during adolescence or further increase chronic disease risk later on in adulthood. Stunting, for instance, originates in foetal life, in infancy or in childhood.
