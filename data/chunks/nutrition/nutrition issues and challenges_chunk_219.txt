In a longitudinal study under way in north-east Brazil (Vieira Bruno and Bailey, 1998), it was shown that becoming a new mother disrupted the education of many girls, and this may be one mechanism for early pregnancy to perpetuate poverty, by interfering with schooling. In this study, it was found that adolescent pregnancy would not be equated with unwanted pregnancy, however: 40% of the prenatal group of adolescents said that they wanted to have a baby when they became pregnant.
