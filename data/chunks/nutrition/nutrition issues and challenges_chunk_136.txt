The authors advocate vitamin A supplementation among adolescents, or else extended supplementation in schools up to 12 years, but this would hardly be sustainable in Malawi since school attendance is so low. Although the risk of vitamin A deficiency tends to decline with age, it is now apparent that it often extends in adolescence and early adulthood (Henning et al, 1991), especially among women (Bloem et al, 1995). Evidence of high deficiency rates in adolescents in Bangladesh and Malawi was just described. In adolescent female factory workers in Bangladesh (Ahmed et al, 1997), it was found that 56.2% had low serum retinol (<1.05 mmol) and 14.2% were deficient (serum retinol <0.7 mmol/l).
