For instance, in southern Africa, a randomized, case-control trial was conducted on vitamin- and mineral-fortified biscuits and drinks given as snacks to primary-school children (van Stuijvenberg et al, 1999). The biscuit was fortified with ß-carotene, iodine and iron ( 50% of the recommended daily allowance), and the drink with vitamin C. After one year, significant improvements in iron, vitamin A and iodine status were observed, and those who needed it benefited most from the intervention. The concept of a (fortified) school snack, rather than a meal, is also interesting as otherwise there may be substitution of school meals for home meals.
