Calcium deficiency apparently increases the risk of bone fracture even among adolescents, as observed among those not meeting 60% of dietary allowance for calcium (Chan et al, 1984). It was reported that milk consumption had an impact on bone and body composition in Caucasian pubertal girls (Chan, Hoffman and McMurray, 1995). However, the findings of this last study raised controversy (Kerstetter and Insogna 1995; Solomons 1996), with the small sample size recognized as a major weakness.
