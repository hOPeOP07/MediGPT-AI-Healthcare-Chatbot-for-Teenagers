Cross-sectional and longitudinal studies in Peruvian children showed that malnutrition, as evidenced by anthropometric status, not only delayed tooth development and affected the age distribution of dental caries, but was also associated with a higher number of carious deciduous and permanent teeth. Even mild or moderate malnutrition during infancy was associated with increased caries in both deciduous and permanent teeth. Dental enamel hypoplasia is also associated with malnutrition in the early years, although it is not a highly specific indicator of nutritional status (Goodman and Rose, 1991).
