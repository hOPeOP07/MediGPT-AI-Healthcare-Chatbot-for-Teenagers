Micronutrient deficiencies and bone disease Calcium requirements for skeletal development appear to be even greater during adolescence than childhood or young adulthood (Matkovic and Ilich, 1993). Because maximum bone mass is acquired N U T R I T I O N I N A D O L E S C E N C E during adolescence, calcium deposited during that period determines the risk of osteoporosis in adulthood. Adverse effects of calcium deficiency have also been observed at adolescence. For instance, it was reportedly associated with bone demineralization in lactating adolescents (Chan et al, 1987), and increasing calcium intake reversed the condition.
