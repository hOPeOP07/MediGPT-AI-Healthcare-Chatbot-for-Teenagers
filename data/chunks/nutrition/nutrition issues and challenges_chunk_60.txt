It is possible that nutritional interventions induce an earlier and accelerated growth spurt, but it is not sure, based on available evidence, that it will affect final attained adult height, since it may then accelerate maturation, with a shortened growth period as a result. Remedial action in adolescence would be considered as coming late compared to that undertaken in infancy, but it may still be relevant, if shown to be effective in increasing lean body mass. However, catch-up growth intervention in adolescence will likely not improve other consequences of early growth retardation, in particular learning impairment (Martorell, Kettel Khan and Schroeder, ibid.).
