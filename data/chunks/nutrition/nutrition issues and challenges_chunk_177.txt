Tracking from childhood through adulthood of cardiovascular risk factors, which tend to cluster together, has been recognised (Freedman et al, 1985; Bao et al, 1994). Since atherosclerosis (and risk factors such as hyperlipidemia) begins in childhood and progresses into adulthood, as shown in many studies including Bogalusa and Muscatine (Lauer and Clarke, 1990), it is important to identify children and adolescents at higher risk based on obesity, upper-body adiposity, a family history and, when possible, current lipid abnormalities.
