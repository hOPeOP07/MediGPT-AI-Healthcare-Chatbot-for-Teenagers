Recent review papers (Gillespie 1997; Kurz 1997; Treffers 1998) are available, and therefore only a summary of nutrition-relevant evidence is presented in this paper. Early pregnancy exposes mother and child to risk owing to potential competition for dietary energy and nutrients, and also because of physiological immaturity of the young mother. Furthermore, the more undernourished or stunted the young mother is, the more immature she may be for her age, and consequently the higher the level of risk. It is estimated that 25% of women have their first child before the age of 20 (Senderowitz 1995). Early pregnancy is a problem worldwide, in high-, middle-, or low-income populations alike.
