The constraints to improving adolescent health that these conditions impose have to be recognized, for health programmes can seldom directly focus on social inequity reduction. Many of these factors also impinge on nutrition, particularly through inadequate access to food, or unhealthy dietary patterns. It is plausible that parents allocate food and other resources differently depending on whether the child is a boy or a girl. Studies on gender discrimination in relation to nutrition have primarily focused on children, and there is more evidence of such discrimination against girls in Asia than in Africa (Walker 1997).
