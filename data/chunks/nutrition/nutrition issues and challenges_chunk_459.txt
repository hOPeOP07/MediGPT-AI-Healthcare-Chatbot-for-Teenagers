Night blindness may be a common occurrence in pregnant women (Christian et al, 1998) and should be detected by antenatal care. In a double-blind cluster randomized trial of weekly supplementation with retinol or b-carotene was lowered by 44% in women receiving retinol or b-carotene (West et al, 1999). The decrease in maternal mortality attributed to obstetric causes appeared more important with b-carotene than with retinol, which could suggest that some benefit is related to antioxidant properties of b-carotene, and which supports a food-based approach to vitamin A status improvement.
