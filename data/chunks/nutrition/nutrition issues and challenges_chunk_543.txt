Data from an international multicentre study on postnatal mother-to-child transmission of HIV-1 (Leroy et al, 1998) provide evidence, although not conclusive, that it may be appropriate to encourage HIV- positive women to consider the option of breastfeeding initially, and to stop earlier than would be done otherwise. Indeed, the study suggested that four months of breastfeeding would be associated with less risk of transmission than more prolonged breastfeeding. Younger mothers or those with few children were also found to be more likely to transmit HIV through breastfeeding than older or higher parity women in Malawi (Miotti et al, 1999).
