Dichotomizing the nutrition problems of industrialized versus developing countries is becoming more irrelevant, particularly for adolescents. In developed countries, new forms of food poverty and poor nutrition are observed, linked with growing social inequities and marginalization. In developing countries, western lifestyles and eating patterns are spreading, bringing with them nutrition-related problems. Furthermore, ‘developing countries’ are a diverse group, and the main nutrition issues are likely similar for the majority of adolescents in countries in transition or in rapidly developing economies, for the more affluent in poor countries, and for adolescents of industrialized countries.
