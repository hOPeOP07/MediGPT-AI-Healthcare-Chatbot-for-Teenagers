Anthropometric assessment of adolescents is more complex than children’s because of the transition in body composition, and of the variable timing of the growth spurt. Although research is ongoing on anthropometric references and cut-off points for body mass index (BMI), it would appear relevant to consider anthropometric assessment of adolescents based on BMI as routine in health care, and perhaps also in school health. Body weight management is seen as a highly relevant means of preventing obesity and eating disturbances in industrialized countries, and it should be the same in developing countries.
