Indeed, it was reported in the USDA Nationwide Food Consumption Survey that snacks provided up to one third of adolescents’ daily energy intake, and a significant amount of micronutrients (Bigler-Doughten and Jenkins, 1987). Fast foods are popular choices among adolescents, and this can contribute to high intakes of saturated fat. Based on NHANES II and the National Adolescent School Health Survey, it was observed that adolescents who obtained breakfast and lunch foods from sources other than home or school were more likely to consume high-fat, low nutrient- dense snacks, as well as dieting over the previous year (Douch et al, 1995).
