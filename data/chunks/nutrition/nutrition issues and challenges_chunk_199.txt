12 Reuters Health, April 29, 1999 Adolescent pregnancy has begun decreasing, at least in the USA (Blum 1998). According to NCHS data12, the US teen pregnancy rate has dropped 17% since peaking in 1990. Between 1995-96, the rate fell by 4%. The decline is attributed to increased use of contraceptives, as well as decreased sexual activity. Yet teenage pregnancy is still widespread. Physical growth, mental and sexual development of girls during adolescence may have a critical effect on their capacity to carry successful pregnancies, and the health and nutritional status of today’s adolescent girls will largely determine the quality of the next generation (Ahmed et al, 1998).
