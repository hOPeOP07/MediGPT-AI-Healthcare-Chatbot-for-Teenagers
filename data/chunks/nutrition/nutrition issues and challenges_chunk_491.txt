The greater the anticipated reinforcement, the greater the likelihood that a behaviour will occur and be sustained. Greater self-worth and social confidence are expected outcomes that were strongly related to the frequency of dieting. Frequent dieting was also associated with lower self-esteem, and with the female sex. Actually, self-esteem was the best predictor of weight-reduction behaviours in general, including diet-pill use and purging.
