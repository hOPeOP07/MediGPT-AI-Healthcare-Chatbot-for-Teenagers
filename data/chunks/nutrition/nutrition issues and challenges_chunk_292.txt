According to the social cognitive theory, a high level of self- efficacy is needed in order to change behaviour. Self-efficacy is enhanced by knowledge, practical skills and social reinforcement. The theory of planned behaviour is an attempt to assess the relationship between attitudes and behaviour. It postulates that behaviour is a function of behavioural intention, which in turn depends upon three main sets of variables: attitudes towards performing the behaviour; social pressure; and for behaviours which are not under full volitional control, perceptions of control over performing the behaviour.
