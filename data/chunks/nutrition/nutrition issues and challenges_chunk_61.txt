However, catch-up growth intervention in adolescence will likely not improve other consequences of early growth retardation, in particular learning impairment (Martorell, Kettel Khan and Schroeder, ibid.). Furthermore, in adolescents who were born small because of intrauterine growth retardation, nutritional intervention for catch-up growth may result in gain in fat rather than in height (Léger and Czernichow, 1999), as such individuals appear more prone to becoming obese. Studies undertaken as part of the adolescent research programme of the International Center for Research in Women (ICRW) contributed to knowledge about the extent of catch-up growth in height during adolescence, in the absence of or with food supplementation (Kurz and Johnson-Welch, 1994).
