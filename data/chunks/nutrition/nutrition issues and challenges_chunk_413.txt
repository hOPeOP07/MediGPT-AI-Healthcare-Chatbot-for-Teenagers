Already in 1996, approximately 60% of the salt consumed in developing countries was iodized, compared to less than 10% just six years earlier (Alnwick, ibid.). However, iodine supplementation as an alternative or complementary intervention will continue to be needed in those endemic areas where iodized salt is not practicable, available or accessible (Solomons 1998), and where alternative fortification measures have not been implemented, for instance iodization of well water (Fisch et al, 1993) or of irrigation water, as in parts of China (DeLong et al, 1997).
