Evidence from supplementation trials suggests that marginal zinc nutriture may also limit skeletal growth in some infants, children and adolescents (King 1996). In Guatemalan infants, zinc supplementation increased accretion of fat-free mass and enhanced linear growth in those that were stunted at baseline (Rivera et al, 1998). Similarly, in a double-blind placebo-controlled study in Viet Nam, growth of stunted children was improved by daily or weekly multi-micronutrient supplements providing zinc, vitamin A, iron and vitamin C (Thu et al, 1999).
