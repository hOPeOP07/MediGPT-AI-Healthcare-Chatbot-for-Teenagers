In another study, girls were more likely to have eaten fruit and vegetables the previous day, and less likely to have eaten high-fat meats and snacks than boys (Dinger and Waigandt, 1997). It is observed that the busy lifestyle of today’s families in the USA often results in adolescents skipping meals or relying on snack foods for basic nutrition. In the national survey of 1987, from 34% to 45% of school teenagers skipped breakfast at least four days a week, almost half reported eating three snacks a day, 60% of this food being high in fat but otherwise low in nutritional quality (American School Health Association 1989).
