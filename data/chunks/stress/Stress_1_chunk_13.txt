Speciﬁc types of anxiety disorders, such as panic disorder (PD), speciﬁc phobias, social phobia, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), acute stress disorder, separation anxiety disorder (SAD), and generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) occur with different frequency during the period of childhood and adolescence. Epidemiological studies show that GAD is the most common, followed by SAD, social phobia, and the speciﬁc phobias. Much less frequent are PTSD, OCD, and PD, with less than 1% prevalence in population samples. Consid- ering gender differences, research shows that girls are affected more, and the overall gender ratio for anxiety disorders throughout childhood and adolescence is w2:1 (Costello et al., 2004).
