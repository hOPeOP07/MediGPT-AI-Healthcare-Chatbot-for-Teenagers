The main-effect model states that social support has independent effects on mental and physical health outcomes regardless of the level of stress experienced, while the stress-buffering model posits that social support has indirect effects on health outcomes by acting as a buffer against stress (Cohen and Wills, 1985). Among adolescents, the social support of family and peers has great signiﬁcance. Parental support has been found to be a predictor of better adjustment and less distress in adoles- cence.
