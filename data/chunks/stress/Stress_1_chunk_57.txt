Social support most effectively acts as a buffer against stress when it matches the needs elicited by a stressful event. Even social relationships that are close and supportive are not uniformly positive and may have negative consequences. For example, being close to and having highly supportive anti- social parents might result in various types of problematic behaviors of adolescents, such as substance abuse or conduct disorder (Rook, 2003). Also, it has been found that diabetic adolescents who turn to their peers for coping strategies may have worse metabolic control than those who turn to their parents (Aldwin, 2007).
