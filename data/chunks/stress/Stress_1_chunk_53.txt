It has been deﬁned broadly as interpersonal social resources that involve either the presence or the implication of stable human relationships. Social support is regarded as structural (e.g., number of social contacts, size, and density of social networks) or functional (e.g., informative, emotional, instrumental). Evidence suggests that social support generally contributes to better adjustment of adolescents and that their low satisfaction with social support is associated with anxiety, depres- sion, psychosomatic symptoms, and interpersonal sensitivity.
