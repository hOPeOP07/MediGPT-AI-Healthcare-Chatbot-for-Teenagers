Adolescent Stress: Causes, Impact, and Coping
Strategies
Naheed Fatima and Shahla Shabeeh Shaheen
Abstract
Adolescence is a developmental stage known for its physical, psychological, and social
transformations, It is frequently accompanied by elevated stress levels. In this review paper the causes,
effects, and coping strategies of stress in adolescents are examined. This study offers practical methods
for managing adolescent stress and encouraging healthy ageing by identifying stressors and their
impacts on mental and physical health. Stressors associated with growing up and becoming an adult
include peer relationships, parental expectations, social media influences, and academic pressure. High
level of stress can have major negative impacts on one's mental and physical health; it leads to anxiety,
despair, and risky behaviour in adolescents. This paper incorporates recent research findings to
comprehensively understand this significant topic through an analysis of the causes, consequences, and
coping strategies related to adolescent stress. Adolescence is a crucial developmental period that is
marked by changes in the body, emotions, and mind. As a person grows from childhood to adulthood
numerous stressors come up such as academic pressure, peer pressure, familial expectations and social
media effects. High level of stress can result in, dangerous behaviours depression, anxiety and other
serious effects on one's physical and mental health. This paper examined the causes, effects, and coping
mechanisms of adolescents’ stress, which incorporates new research findings to offer a thorough grasp
of this important topic.
Keywords: Adolescents stress, causes, impact, coping strategies
1. Introduction
Adolescence is a significant stage of physical, emotional, and intellectual development, as
well as significant changes in society (Petersen, 2021) [32]. While these changes are important
for the development of identity and independence, they often cause problems for young
peoples ‘mental health. The pressures experienced during this time are many; these include
the strong need for academic achievement, the difficulty of social adjustment, and the
challenge of finding one’s own identity (Belcher et al., 2021) [4]. Modern influences such as
global media add to these challenges by bringing constant comparison, fear of missing out,
and the pressure to get the best online photos (McCrory et al., 2022) [23]. These stressors,
when combined with typical developmental issues, put adolescents at high risk for mental
health problems such as anxiety, depression, and low self-esteem. Understanding the history
of these stressors and their impact on mental health is crucial for creating effective strategies
and solutions (Auttama et al., 2022) [3]. This understanding not only help adolescents manage
stress; but also provide techniques to build resilience and adaptability when they face future
challenges. According to Sawyer et al. (2018) [37], adolescence is a period of transition
between childhood and adulthood marked by rapid changes in cognitive, physical, social,
and emotional domains. If these changes are not properly addressed then, they frequently
cause stress, which can lead to constant issues with one's physical and mental health.
Adolescents experience stress for a variety of reasons, such as peer relationships, family
issues, cultural challenges, and academic expectations. In order to create effective coping
strategies, it is necessary to recognize the causes and effects of adolescent stress.
1.1 Objectives
1. To identify causes of stress in adolescents.
2. To identify the impact of stress on adolescents.
3. To provide different stress management strategies for adolescents..

2. Causes of Adolescent Stress
Adolescents encounter stress from various sources. Key
causes are depicted in Fig.1 and described below:
Fig 1: Causes of Adolescent Stress
2.1 Academic Pressure: Academic pressure is one of the
main causes of stress in adolescents. The pressure to fulfil
expectations of parents and teachers, to perform well on
exams, and ensure a bright future frequently leads to stress
and burnout. A major source of stress for adolescents is
academic expectations and competition for admission to
high schools and colleges which can be overwhelming as
documented in previous studies (Luthar et al.,2020) [21]. One
of the most frequent causes of stress among adolescents is
pressure from school, it is further increased by building up
pressure to excel in competitive exams, tests and
professional aspirations (Deb et al., 2015) [11]. Anxiety and
burnout are the results of adolescents’ stage that creates
imbalance in their personal lives, extracurricular activities,
and schoolwork.
2.2 Social Expectations and Peer Pressure: During
adolescence, social connections are essential. Peer pressure,
friendship issues, and romantic relationship problems are
common sources of stress. Adolescents may feel pressure to
follow group principles, even if they violate their personal
beliefs. Peer pressure is crucial in adolescents' decision-
making processes that can result in risk-taking behaviours
and it increase stress levels in adolescents (Prinstein and
Giletta, 2016) [33]. Many adolescents feel pressure to adjust
with their peers and experiencing stress about appearance
lifestyle choices, and popularity (Papageorgiou et al.,2022).
Bullying, peer pressure, and the need to adjust can cause
mental pain, risky behaviours and low self- esteem. Making
friendships, peer approval and social interactions can be a
major source of stress in adolescents (Prinstein and Giletta,
2016) [33].
2.3 Family Dynamics: Conflicts within the family or
pressure to meet parental expectations can lead to
considerable stress (Jiang et al.,2022) [16]. The dynamics of
the family are a great aspect in adolescent’s stress.
According to Sirin et al. (2016)
[39], High parental
expectations, family disputes and lack of communication
can result in emotional distress. Adolescents experience
higher levels of stress who are from broken family or those
who are going through parental divorce.
2.4 Identity Formation and Self-Esteem
Individuals struggle with identity formation and issues
related to self-esteem during this age. Concerns about one's
own value, physical appearance, and future goals can lead to
internal stress, particularly when combined with principles
promoted by society and the media.
Erikson's psychosocial theory discovers "identity vs. role
confusion" as a major adolescent struggle (Erikson, 1968)
[12], emphasizing the psychological stress caused by identity
exploration.
2.5 Digital Influences: Growing usage of social media
caused digital stress in adolescents. Fear of missing out
(FOMO), online comparison, cyberbullying, and the need to
maintain a digital persona can all cause anxiety in
adolescents. Social media usage has been linked to stress, as
adolescents are exposed to cyberbullying and often develop
unrealistic expectations based on curated content (Ajewumi
et al., 2024)
[1]. According to Keles et al. (2020)
[17],
adolescents who use social media more regularly are more
likely to experience stress and anxiety due to ongoing social
comparison,
unattainable
beauty
standards,
and
cyberbullying. The pressure to maintain a well-curated
online presence may lead to emotional exhaustion and
issues with self-esteem. Orben et al. (2019), found a
significant correlation between digital media use and mental
health problems, especially among female adolescents.
Hormonal changes in adolescents allow them to control
their emotions and mood.
2.6 Biological and Hormonal Changes: These changes can
make adolescents more vulnerable to stress, particularly if
they struggle to understand and manage their emotions.
According to Casey et al. (2010) [6], these neurobiological
changes during adolescence increase emotional sensitivity
and prolong stress reactions.
2.7 Social and Economic Difficulties: Social stigma,
limited access to resources, and financial instability are
additional stressors that often affect adolescents from low-
income families or marginalized communities. According to
Evans and Cassells (2014) [13], Adolescents' cognitive and
emotional development affects negatively through ongoing
stress linked to poverty. A complex interaction of
biological, social, familial, academic, and environmental
factors caused stress in adolescents. Parents, teachers, and
mental health professionals must understand these issues in
order to provide appropriate support and solutions.
3. Impact of Stress on Adolescents
Adolescents face many changes in their social, mental, and
physical life during this age. During this age stress might
lead to major issues that endure a long time. Peer pressure,
familial problems, educational pressures, or personal
concerns are the main causes of stress in adolescents and it
affect all aspects of an adolescent's life.

3.1 Effects on the Mind and Emotions: Adolescents stress
is related to various mental and emotional problems, like
anxiety, depression, low self-esteem and mood swings.
According to studies adolescents, who experience regular
stress that has been suffered from mental health issues like
major depressive disorder and generalised anxiety disorder.
According to studies, adolescents who experience regular
stress often show signs like restlessness, impatience, or
long-term depression.
3.2 Effects on Academic Performance and Cognitive
Function: Adolescents who are experience more stress face
challenges to think properly, particularly regarding to
memory, concentration, and decisions. This can affect their
academic performance and motivation. Excessive stress
produces high level of cortisol that can harm the
hippocampus, a brain part involved in memory and learning
(Lupien et al., 2009) [20]. Students, who experience more
stress struggle with focus, problem-solving, and academic
success (Deb et al., 2015) [11]. Long-term stress can cause
academic difficulties, social withdrawal and dangerous
habits like substance use. Adolescents who experience more
stress it can also lead to self-harm and alcohol addiction.
3.3 Implications for Physical Health: Stress can show up
physically, causing headaches, sleep problems, and
weakened immunity (Chu et al., 2024) [8]. It reduces
immunity, making it easier to get sick. Stress is also
associated to headaches, tension, stomach issues, and sleep
difficulties (Romeo, 2017) [34]. Adolescents who are under
stress may develop harmful behaviours, such as substance
abuse or poor diet, which can lead to eating disorders or
obesity.
The body can suffer from stress, leading to headaches,
stomach pain, exhaustion, and sleep problems. Stress
triggers the HPA axis, which controls the body’s response to
stress and can harm the immune system if overactive
(McEwen, 2006). Adolescents under stress often also have
sleep issues and unhealthy eating habits (Owens et al.,
2017) [29].
3.4 Behavioural Problems and Taking Chances:
Adolescents who are under stress are more likely to use
unhealthy coping strategies like substance use or social
withdrawal. Adolescents who are more stressed are more
susceptible to peer pressure, which can lead to harmful
activities like drug use, unprotected sex, or careless driving
(Prinstein and Giletta, 2016) [33]. These actions may provide
short-term relief but can increase stress and cause serious
chronic issues.
3.5 Relationships and Social Functioning: Stress can
negatively
affect
social
skills
and
relationship.
Adolescents who experience more stress may engage in
arguments, act out, or detach themselves from peers.
According to La Greca and Harrison (2005) [19], Adolescents
who experience rejection or bullying by peers that leads to
social stress, which can cause anxiety and feelings of
loneliness.
3.6 Long-Term Developmental Effects: Adolescents who
are under stress may experience mental health issues and
delay
the
development
throughout
adulthood.
Adolescents who experience long-term stress, it leads to
substance abuse, chronic diseases and mental illness, later in
life (Felitti et al., 1998) [14]. Adolescent stress might affect a
person's general health eventually. There are numerous
effects of stress on adolescents' behaviour, social
development, physical health, emotions, and thought
processes. Stress can hinder with healthy growth and have
long-term impacts in the absence of appropriate assistance
and coping mechanisms. It requires early intervention,
supportive surroundings, and stress-reduction techniques to
protect the health of teenagers.
4.Coping Strategies
Academic
responsibilities,
social
pressures,
identity
problems and hormonal changes lead to stress in
adolescents. At this stage, it’s critical to manage stress to
protect mental health and prevent harmful effects. The
following strategies can help adolescents to manage stress
more effectively.
4.1 Cognitive-Behavioural Therapy (CBT) Techniques:
CBT help adolescents in recognising and transforming
negative thought patterns. It teaches adolescents to
challenge harmful beliefs and create more effective coping
mechanisms. CBT enhances emotional regulation and
problem-solving skills, which helps with anxiety and
depression.
For example, engaging in a 10-minute guided meditation or
practicing mindful breathing might help lower tension and
increase focus.
4.2 Exercise and Physical Activity
Doing exercise often helps to reduce stress. It makes your
body to release endorphins, which are chemicals that make
you feel-good and reduce stress hormones. Adolescents can
play team sports, do yoga, or even take regular walks to feel
better and manage stress. According to the Centres for
Disease Control and Prevention (CDC, 2022), young people
should engage in moderate-to-intense physical activities for
at least 60 minutes each day to maintain the health of their
bodies and minds.
4.3 Setting Goals and Managing Time:
If adolescents learn how to set goals and manage their time,
they can be more productive and feel less stressed
(Zimmerman, 2002) [44]. Adolescents can better balance
school work, extra activities, and free time, if they are good
at managing time, it helps reduce stress from last-minute
problems. For example, planning study time and breaks in a
weekly planner can help avoid getting tired and putting
things off. According to research by Misra and McKean
(2000) [26], students experience less stress and do better
academically, who are good at managing their time.
4.4 Social Support Systems
Having good relations with family, friends and teachers give
support and comfort during tough times. These relations
have the potential to lessen the negative consequences of
stress and increase resilience. For example, talking to a
friend or family members about a difficult situation can give
new ideas and a sense of being understood. Research shows
that adolescents with high levels of social support
experience lower levels of stress and sadness (Rueger et al.,
2016) [35].

4.5 Mindfulness Techniques: Teenagers can learn to
regulate their emotions by practicing deep breathing and
meditation (Broderick, 2021) [5]. Yoga and mindful writing
are two techniques that can help lower stress levels.
4.6 Limiting Social Media Use
Encouraging adolescents to spend more time offline and
take breaks from social media can help improve their mental
health and reduce stress. Parents are crucial, to observe their
teenagers'
screen
time
and
encourage
responsible
technology use.
Through hobbies like drama, art, singing, and journaling,
adolescents can release tension and express their emotions
in a healthy way. For example, A student who enjoys
drawing or sketching, can utilise art journaling to
communicate depressive or anxious feelings. Research by
Stuckey and Nobel (2010) [40] indicates that creative
activities can improve mood, reduce stress and help mental
health.
4.7 Healthy Lifestyle Choices
Getting enough sleep, eating well, and staying hydrated are
important for managing stress. Poor habits can make stress
more severe and emotions more unstable. For example,
adolescents who have a regular sleep schedule and avoid
coffee at night feel happier and handle stress better.
According to Owens et al. (2017) [29], adolescents who sleep
regularly experience less stress and do better in school.
4.8 School-Based Programs
School programs that help reduce stress often focus on
teaching coping skills, stress management, and mental
health education. These programs may include support
groups, cognitive-behavioural techniques, mindfulness, and
relaxation methods. Some schools also use wellness centres
to offer activities like yoga and exercise. According to
Hoover and Bostic (2021) [15], schools are crucial because
they provide mental health support, stress management
techniques, and therapy.
5. Conclusion
Stress in adolescents is caused by both external influences
and personal growth. By identifying the causes of stress and
employing practical coping mechanisms, adolescents can
lessen negative consequences and make a smooth transition
into adulthood. Further research into specific programs
might improve coping methods and help adolescents’
mental health. Adolescents’ stress is influenced by social,
family, academic, and digital factors. Too much stress
without proper handling can harm both physical and mental
health. On the other hand, effective coping strategies like
managing time, having strong support, using cognitive
techniques, and adopting healthy habits can lessen these
effects. Parents, teachers, and mental health professionals
should work together to create support systems that help
adolescents build resilience and feel well.
